# Labeling the real library: five strategies compared, 2026-11-21

An agent organised the 7,159 saved X bookmarks into a tag tree through Kosha's pipeline (`POST /api/query`, and the `tags` and `runs` tools at `/mcp`), tried five strategies in separate label runs, revised the tree once and promoted the best result into `main` as proposed tags. No bookmark text is reproduced here: only paths, definitions, stems, author handles and counts.

**What is in `main` now:** 32 tags, all `proposed`, all agent-made; 11,344 agent-attached tag rows on 5,841 items (81.6% of the library has a primary tag; 3,148 also have a secondary). 1,318 items are untagged. Nothing was approved. Undo: `runs {action: "undo", batch: 215}`. (`main` also holds a `zz-kosha-test` tree of 4 tags and 89 rows that another process created during the night; this run did not touch it.)

**The reference is an LLM, not the owner.** Agreement figures below compare each strategy with `gpt-6.2-sol` reading the item's text (up to 6,000 characters) for 200 random items (`all | sample 200 42`). It first described each item freely in 3 to 6 words, then picked the best path from each candidate tree, or "none". The ids, the free descriptions and the picks are in `labeling_reference_200.json` (no item text) for the owner to correct. A strategy agreeing with this reference agrees with another model; 200 items also means one item is half a point.

## 3. Results

Coverage is the share of the 7,159 saved items with a primary tag. Agreement is over all 200 reference items (an untagged item agrees when the reference said "none"). Precision is over the tagged reference items only: the primary is the reference's primary or one of its "others". Jev $ is what the strategy would cost with an empty cache (strategies shared cached answers; the billed figure is in section 7). Tokens are the characters of every tool reply divided by 4. Wall time is server time in the calls, without the agent's own thinking.

| Strategy | Tree | Coverage | Exact | Parent level | Precision of primaries | Primaries at 0.8 to 0.9 | Jev $ | Tokens read | Calls | Wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Top-down seeded, question style 1 | v1 | 41.1% | 43.5% | 45.5% | 98.9% | 1,586 | $0.147 | 14,800 | 205 | 25 s |
| A2. The same with question style 2, plus parents asked of leftovers | v1 | 75.6% | 61.0% | 68.5% | 81.1% | 2,112 | $0.168 | 16,800 | 214 | 19 s |
| B. Bottom-up groups (30 groups named, members verified, two tags split) | B | 47.5% | 47.0% | 52.0% | 90.3% | 1,525 | $0.115 | 18,300 (+ the map) | 85 | 10 s |
| C. Author-first (63 authors mapped to 20 tags); stopped at the slice of authors with 12 or more items | v1 | 12.4% | 17.0% | 18.5% | 90.3% | 385 | $0.027 | 10,100 | 106 | 3 s |
| D. `hybrid "<definition>" \| head 400 \| jev … \| tag` per tag | v1 | 34.6% | 38.5% | 40.5% | 93.6% | 836 | $0.059 | 11,400 | 67 | 14 s |
| E. `all \| score_labels apply` | v1 | 79.1% | 67.0% | 74.0% | 88.5% | 1,734 | $0.39 | 2,900 | 11 | 31 s |
| **F. Final: `score_labels` on tree v2, then a label-centred leftover round at 0.9** | v2 | **81.6%** | **76.0%** | **82.5%** | **94.2%** | 1,423 | $0.45 | 12,500 | 127 | 64 s |

Reading the table:

- **E and F win.** The item-centred loop has the best coverage and the best agreement, and needs the fewest tokens: one call, one summary. It costs two to three times the Jev money of A2, which here is 20 cents.
- **The tree and the wording mattered more than the strategy.** The same `score_labels` call went from 67% to 75% exact, and from 88.5% to 97% precision, on the 200 by revising the tree (before the leftover round). Strategy A went from 41% to 76% coverage by rewording the questions.
- **A2 is cheap and wide but loose.** Each tag is asked alone, so a broad question (`AI Products & Tools`, `AI/LLM App Frameworks`) takes items that a narrower tag should own; the primary is only the best of the tags that happened to retrieve the item. `score_labels` asks every item about eight competing tags, which is why its primaries are better.
- **D is A with a worse candidate step.** `hybrid` with a sentence-long definition returned 24 to 200 items for most tags, not 400 (the keyword half of the fusion narrows it), so recall is capped. `semantic "…" top N` is the stage to use for candidates.
- **B finds the topics but cannot cover the library.** Only half of the group members pass the question written for their own group; the rest belong to another tag and are never asked about it. Its tree had no place for 17 of the 200 reference items (8 to 10 for the hand-written trees). Use `groups` for the map and for leftovers, not as the labeler.
- **C is dominated.** Authors are less single-topic than their terms suggest: 889 of 2,073 items (43%) from the 63 "single-topic" authors passed their author's tag. It covers 12% and is right when it speaks, so `from:` belongs in step 5a as one more source of candidates, nothing more.

Per-tag results for A2 and the leftover round are in the run namespaces `run-4` and `run-9`; `runs compare` prints coverage and spend side by side for runs 3 to 9.

## 4. The Jev question style that worked

Style 1, as the playbook suggested (the definition, with what counts and what does not):

> Is this post mainly about user interface design or frontend development: Figma, design systems, landing pages, React, Next.js, Tailwind, shadcn, animations and UI inspiration?

On the 300 nearest items this put **0** at 0.8 or above. Variants on the same 300 items:

|---|---|---|
| "Is this post about user interface design or frontend web development?" | 14 | 0 |
| "Is the topic of this post UI design or frontend development?" | 3 | 0 |
| "Does this post belong in a folder named Design and Frontend, which holds posts on …?" | 43 | 17 |
| "Is this post about designing or building user interfaces?" | 48 | 20 |
| **"Is this post about designing or building user interfaces, for example Figma, landing pages, React components, Tailwind, shadcn or UI animations?"** | 50 | 34 |

The same happened for India ("India or Tamil Nadu, for example …": 47 at 0.8; "India, Indians or Indian culture, politics, religion or economy": 198).

Rules, each seen in the histograms:

1. **One head concept.** "about X or Y" with two different head nouns is read as a claim that is half true, and everything lands in 0.5 to 0.8. Fold the second noun into the examples or into one phrase ("designing or building user interfaces").
2. **Then "for example" and concrete things** a post would mention (tool names, formats). The examples raise true items from 0.8 to 0.9; they did not lower any histogram.
3. **No "mainly", no "the topic of".** Both push everything down.
4. **No exclusions in the question** ("not coding assistants …"). Keep tags apart with competing tags and `roles auto`, not with a negative clause.
5. **A full sentence ending in a question mark**, as the smoke run already found.
6. A tag's stored definition follows the same shape: "Posts about <head concept>, for example <things>." `score_labels` builds its question from it.

The style is looser: A2's precision against the reference was 81% where style 1 had 99% on the few items it tagged. In the item-centred loop the competition between tags takes that looseness back out (94 to 97%).

## 5. The recommended algorithm

For an agent with Kosha's `query`, `tags` and `runs` tools. Budget for 7,000 items: $0.50 of Jev, about 15,000 tokens of tool replies.

1. **Start a run.** `runs {action: "start", name, strategy, budget_usd: 0.7}`. Pass `run` to every `query` and `tags` call. Never write to `main` directly.
2. **Map, about 6,000 tokens, $0.**
   - `all | stats`, `all | count by type`, `all | count by folder top 30`, `all | count by author top 40`, `all | count by domain top 30`, `all | count by year`, `all | count by lang top 8`.
   - `all | groups 30 as m`, then `@m<n> | terms 9` for every group. Read the terms before the samples.
   - `@m<n> | groups 5 as m<n>.` only for groups with cohesion under about 0.66.
   - A folder with fewer than 20 items is a name to keep, not a candidate set.
3. **Write the tree: 15 to 20 top-level tags, two levels.** One `tags` call with `create` operations.
   - Definition: "Posts about <one head concept>, for example <concrete things>." (section 4).
   - A parent that only groups its children gets **no definition**: `score_labels` then skips it. A parent that is a topic of its own (`Career` above `Career/Immigration & Visas`) gets one.
   - Put a tag under the parent it would otherwise compete with.
   - Keep the owner's folder names as tags even when they will be small.
4. **Dry run on 300, 2 cents.** `all | sample 300 7 | score_labels` (confirm). Read: placed of 300 (expect 80% or more), the largest labels, the overlap lines. Fix the tree if one tag holds over a quarter, if two siblings share more than half of the smaller, or if a tag you expected is missing from the top ten.
5. **Full pass.** `all | score_labels apply`. It asks for a spend confirm, then a write confirm: three calls, about $0.05 per 1,000 items, 30 seconds for 7,000. If the call fails midway, send it again: the answers that arrived are cached, and the error says what was spent.
6. **Read the hints once.** A too-broad or overlapping tag: revise definitions (`tags` `describe`), undo the batch (`runs undo`), run step 5 again. One revision only; each full pass costs the same again because a reworded definition is a new question.
7. **Leftover round, label-centred, at 0.9.**
   - `where is:untagged | as @left | count`
   - per defined tag: `@left | semantic "<head concept>: <examples>" top 400 | jev "Is this post about <head concept>, for example <examples>?" >= 0.9 | tag "<path>"`
   - per container parent, last: `where is:untagged | jev "Is this post about <parent topic>?" >= 0.9 | tag "<parent>"`
   - `all | roles auto`
   - Use 0.9 here, not 0.8: these items already failed the item-centred pass, and at 0.8 the round added 12 reference items of which 6 matched even at parent level; at 0.9 it added 7 and 6 matched.
8. **Look for a missing topic, one round.** `where is:untagged | groups 12 as u`. For a group with a nameable subject, write a question and count first: `where is:untagged | jev "<question>" >= 0.9 as x | count`. Add the tag only if it would hold more than 10% of the untagged. Here four candidates held 33 to 79 of 1,318, so none was added.
9. **Stop** when a leftover round tags under 10% of what was untagged, or coverage passes 85%, or the next call would pass the budget. Report `where is:untagged | stats` and `where is:untagged is:thin | count`: what is left is mostly posts whose content is in the image or video.
10. **Close and promote.** `runs {action: "close"}`, then `runs {action: "promote"}` twice (preview, then with `confirm`). Tags arrive in `main` as `proposed` with agent-attached rows; approve nothing.

Cheap-first ordering inside any label-centred step: `where` / `grep` / `from:` (free) → `semantic … top N` (free, one query embedding) → `terms 6` on the candidate set as a sanity check (90 tokens) → `jev` (about $0.007 per 1,000 items) → `tag`.

New items each day: `where is:untagged saved:1d.. | score_labels apply`.

## 6. What kept the context small

- **Counts, not rows.** End a set-building call with `| count`; without `show` or a summary stage the reply carries five sample rows (about 100 tokens) that nobody needs. A per-tag chain (candidates, union, terms, jev, tag preview, tag) is about 450 tokens.
- **One chained call per tag** for the leftover round: `@left | semantic … | jev … >= 0.9 | tag …` returns the histogram and the write result in about 110 tokens.
- **`score_labels` is the smallest of all**: the full library in 11 calls and 2,900 tokens, of which 1,900 were the `tags` reply that echoes the tree.
- **`terms` instead of samples** to name a group or check a candidate set: nine stems with counts in 90 tokens.
- **The `tags` reply is the largest single thing read** (about 1,900 tokens for 32 definitions). Call it once after creating the tree, not after every write; use `runs status` (60 tokens) for progress.
- **Never page through `show`.** The 200 reference ids were the only rows fetched, by a script.
- The whole experiment, seven runs and the map, read about 96,000 tokens of tool replies. The recommended algorithm alone is about 6,000 for the map and 12,500 for the final run.

## 8. What went wrong

1. **The full `score_labels` pass failed midway both times** (56,000 questions in about 7,000 requests; some request failed after 18 seconds). The answers that had arrived were cached, so the retry asked only the last 4,000, but the first time the paid part (about $0.37) reached no ledger row: `kosha.jev.ask` writes the row only after a clean return. Fixed in the pipeline (below); the second time the ledger shows a `score_labels_failed` row of $0.344. Why a request fails at this size was not found. A single `jev` stage also failed once with an SSL error and passed on retry.
2. **Question style 1 tagged nothing for two real topics** (section 4). The playbook's advice to put "what does not count" into the question was wrong for Jev.
3. **`hybrid` with a long definition returns few items** (24 for India, 47 for investing). This is `kosha/search/`, not changed here.
4. **The confirm threshold of 2,000 new questions** is passed by the 300-item dry run of `score_labels` (2,400 questions, 1.6 cents). An extra round trip, no harm.
5. **A closed run cannot be queried.** `where is:untagged | groups` on a finished run answers "No running label run"; look at leftovers before `close`.
6. **`groups 30` is cut at the reply cap** after 27 groups; the last three need `@m28 | sample 3 | show text:60`.
7. **The HTTP body field is `q`**, not `pipeline` (`POST /api/query {"q": …, "confirm": …, "run": …}`); `runs` has no REST route and was called through `/mcp` with a `tools/call` JSON-RPC body.
8. **The tests refuse any database but `kosha_test`** (`tests/conftest.py` asserts the name), so the lab database `kosha_test_lab` could only be used through a copy of the conftest loaded as a plugin from outside the repository. The conftest was not edited.

### Pipeline changes (`backend/kosha/pipeline/executor.py`, tests in `tests/test_pipeline.py`, 38 pass)

- **A Jev call that fails midway now records what it spent** (a ledger row `<operation>_failed`, with the run id) and the error says how much was spent and that the same call will ask only the rest.
- **`score_labels` skips a parent tag that has children and no definition.** It used to ask about its bare name, which passes for most of the library.
